4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) enables real-time rendering of complex dynamic scenes with high fidelity, but its trajectory representation remains expensive to store. Recent rate-distortion optimized methods substantially reduce this cost, yet important dynamic components remain largely uncompressed. In particular, position wavelet approximation coefficients and rotation keyframes account for over 30% of the total bitstream. The key challenge is not simply to quantize these trajectory attributes, but to first put them into a form that can be quantized effectively. In their native form, these signals carry structure that is benign for representation but detrimental to codebook learning: wavelet approximation coefficient magnitudes vary by orders of magnitude across Gaussians, from near-static to fast-moving points, risking severe gradient imbalance during quantization-aware training if not explicitly conditioned, while absolute rotations and positions retain predictable temporal variation.

We present TAC-4DGS, a trajectory-aware compression method that reparameterizes these components before quantization. A data-adaptive logarithmic companding transform balances wavelet approximation coefficient scales and enables stable end-to-end codebook training, while inter-frame delta rotation coding and reference-frame position subtraction remove predictable structure before quantization. TAC-4DGS achieves 41.9% average bitstream reduction on N3V and 33.8% on Technicolor relative to state-of-the-art 4DGS compression, with PSNR differences of only −0.31 dB and +0.40 dB respectively. As a coding-level extension requiring no changes to the underlying rendering pipeline, TAC-4DGS achieves a 93.6–99.1% total size reduction relative to the uncompressed 4DGS representation.

How it works

In RD4DGS, position wavelet approximation coefficients and rotation keyframes make up over 30% of the bitstream but skip rate-distortion optimized coding entirely. TAC-4DGS brings them under entropy-constrained vector quantization (ECVQ).

Logarithmic magnitude companding

Wavelet approximation coefficients range from near-static to fast-moving Gaussians, over orders of magnitude. Quantized naively, this leads to gradient imbalances of up to 47 000× during training. A median-calibrated μ-law compander evens out the scale so the codebook trains stably end to end.

Inter-frame δ-rotation coding

Instead of independent absolute quaternions, each keyframe stores its rotation relative to the previous one, qk−1−1·qk. These deltas cluster around the identity, so codebook entries go to actual motion rather than global orientation.

Reference-frame delta coding

Subtracting each Gaussian's reference position before the Haar wavelet removes the global scene layout from the trajectory and shrinks the dynamic range the quantizer has to cover.

TAC-4DGS works purely at the coding level: the 4DGS scene representation, training procedure and renderer stay unchanged, so decoded scenes render exactly as fast as before.
